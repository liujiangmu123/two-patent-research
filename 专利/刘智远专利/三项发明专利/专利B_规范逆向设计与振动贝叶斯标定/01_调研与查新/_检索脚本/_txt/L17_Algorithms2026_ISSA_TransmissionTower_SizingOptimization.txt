Abstract
To balance the requirements of structural safety and economic efficiency in transmission
tower design, this study proposes a structural optimization method based on an Improved
Sparrow Search Algorithm (ISSA). Specifically, the methodology integrates Circle chaotic
mapping to initialize the sparrow population distribution, followed by the introduction of a
firefly perturbation strategy. This strategy updates the sparrow positions derived from the
basic algorithm, thereby enhancing the algorithm’s ability to escape local optima. Based on
these enhancements, a mathematical model for structural optimization is established with
the primary objective of minimizing the total weight of the tower. The penalty function
method is employed to handle constraints, while the finite element method is utilized
for internal force analysis. To verify the effectiveness of the proposed approach, a typical
cat-head transmission tower is selected as a case study. The optimization results obtained
from the ISSA are compared with those produced by the Daoheng software, demonstrating
a weight reduction of 3.79%. These findings indicate that the ISSA effectively improves
the optimization performance and provides an efficient solution for the preliminary sizing
design of transmission towers.
Keywords: Improved Sparrow Search Algorithm; transmission tower; finite element
analysis; structure optimization
1. Introduction
Currently, China is undergoing a critical phase in the accelerated development of
a new type of energy system. Consequently, the construction scale of high-voltage and
ultra-high-voltage long-distance power transmission projects is expanding annually. As
the most critical supporting structures in power transmission lines, transmission towers
primarily withstand various loads from transmission lines and the external environment.
Therefore, these structures are characterized by stringent safety requirements and represent
a significant portion of the total project expenditure. In light of the “dual carbon” strategy
related to carbon peaking and carbon neutrality, optimizing the structure of transmission
towers to reduce steel consumption while ensuring structural safety is of significant impor-
tance. This approach not only enhances the economic efficiency of engineering projects but
also contributes substantially to the reduction of carbon emissions.
Structural optimization is generally categorized into size optimization, shape opti-
mization, and topology optimization [1–3]. The core components of this process involve the

establishment of a rigorous mathematical model and the selection of a suitable optimization
algorithm. A mathematical model typically comprises three fundamental elements: design
variables, objective functions, and constraints. The available optimization methodologies
are generally categorized into three classes, namely analytical methods, numerical methods,
and intelligent optimization algorithms [4]. Analytical methods involve complex solution
procedures and are generally unsuitable for large-scale and complex structural optimization
problems in practical engineering. Numerical methods for structural topology and sizing
optimization problems are primarily classified into two categories, namely mathemati-
cal programming methods and optimality criteria methods. Mathematical programming
methods are rooted in mathematical optimization theory and function by converting the
optimization problem into an extremum problem of a mathematical model, which is subse-
quently solved using iterative algorithms. Although characterized by high computational
accuracy and theoretical rigor, mathematical programming methods often entail a sig-
nificant computational burden when applied to complex structures, which hinders their
direct implementation in large-scale engineering. In contrast, optimality criteria meth-
ods are developed by constructing heuristic iterative rules grounded in physical intuition
or mathematical optimality conditions. While these methods exhibit superior computa-
tional efficiency, they are predominantly limited to addressing optimization problems with
simple constraints.
Intelligent optimization algorithms have undergone rapid development in recent years.
These algorithms are inspired by natural phenomena or biological behaviors, including
genetic evolution, the collective interactions of ant colonies, and the foraging strategies
of whales [5–7]. Characterized by strong robustness and high flexibility in diverse ap-
plications, these meta-heuristic approaches are frequently employed to address complex
structural optimization challenges. Numerous scholars have conducted extensive research
on the structural optimization of transmission towers, with their research efforts primarily
focusing on the following two dimensions: The first dimension involves enhancing the
performance of meta-heuristic algorithms and ensuring their suitability for practical engi-
neering. Given that the structural optimization of transmission towers is characterized by
high dimensionality and nonlinearity, researchers frequently integrate biological behav-
ioral patterns observed in nature to augment the efficiency of the optimization process.
Scholars such as Tort et al. [8] and Couceiro et al. [9] enhanced the simulated annealing
algorithm and integrated it into professional design software to facilitate the rapid iterative
calculation of transmission tower structures. Cheng and Shi [10] proposed an improved
fruit fly optimization algorithm to address cross-sectional size optimization problems,
which significantly enhances search accuracy within complex constraint environments.
Baykaso˘glu et al. [11] introduced a weighted superposition attraction algorithm based
on swarm intelligence to optimize complex truss structures, successfully achieving the
minimization of total structural weight. The second dimension focuses on the refinement of
mechanical analysis models and the formulation of effective constraint-handling strategies.
Since the optimization of transmission towers necessitates the consideration of multiple
intricate constraints including structural strength, stability, and stiffness, the fidelity of
the mechanical model is of paramount importance because it directly influences the pre-
cision and feasibility of the final optimization results. Jahangiri et al. [12] implemented
a Prize-Penalty Strategy to manage constraints such as stress and deflection during the
optimization of truss structures, which yielded satisfactory results. Furthermore, the super-
vised classification machine learning method introduced by Rosso et al. [13] demonstrates
excellent adaptability to both nonlinear and discontinuous boundaries.
In response to the increasing complexity and expanding scale of practical engineering
structures, a growing number of researchers have shifted their focus toward intelligent

optimization algorithms. Specifically, the Sparrow Search Algorithm (SSA) [14] is a swarm
intelligence optimization method developed by simulating the natural behaviors of spar-
row populations, such as foraging, competition, and anti-predation. The algorithm is
characterized by high convergence accuracy and a streamlined parameter configuration,
offering significant advantages in addressing optimization problems that are non-linear,
multimodal, and high-dimensional [15]. Due to the challenges of non-uniform population
distribution and the susceptibility of the SSA to premature convergence toward local op-
tima, this study improves the original algorithm by incorporating Circle chaotic mapping
and a firefly perturbation strategy. Building upon these considerations, this study proposes
an Improved Sparrow Search Algorithm (ISSA). In conjunction with the finite element
analysis of transmission tower structures, the enhanced algorithm is applied to structural
optimization with the primary objective of minimizing the total weight of the tower. The
results demonstrate that the improved algorithm can be effectively applied to the structural
optimization design of transmission towers.
Although Circle chaotic mapping and firefly perturbation are both existing strategies,
the focus of this study lies in developing a stage-wise collaborative improvement frame-
work for engineering-constrained optimization problems, tailored to the characteristics
of transmission tower structural optimization. Based on the basic SSA, Circle chaotic
mapping is introduced to alleviate the problem of uneven initial population distribution,
while the firefly perturbation strategy is employed to enhance the local search capability
of SSA during the optimization process and improve its ability to escape local optima.
These two strategies address the limitations of SSA at different optimization stages. Based
on this mechanism, the proposed ISSA is further integrated with finite element analysis,
discrete section selection, and stress, slenderness ratio, and displacement constraints of
transmission towers, and is applied to the preliminary sizing optimization of primary
load-carrying members.
2. Internal Force Analysis of Transmission Towers
The structural optimization of transmission towers is an iterative procedure that
begins with the analysis of internal forces, followed by the selection of member cross-
sections. This sequence is performed repeatedly until a global optimal solution is identified.
Consequently, internal force analysis represents a pivotal stage in the overall optimization
design. For the internal force calculations of the transmission tower structures [16,17],
the finite element method is employed alongside the robust computational capabilities of
MATLAB R2022a to carry out the structural simulations.
2.1. Finite Element Analysis
Finite element analysis is fundamentally categorized into three primary stages, which
include discretization, local element analysis, and global structural analysis. For trans-
mission tower structures, discretization involves representing the individual structural
members as discrete elements, while the junctions where these members intersect and their
connections to the supports are abstracted as nodes. Local element analysis involves the
derivation of the mechanical relationship between nodal forces and nodal displacements
for each individual member to formulate the element stiffness matrix. Global structural
analysis involves aggregating the individual stiffness matrices of all members into a com-
prehensive global stiffness matrix by utilizing coordinate transformation matrices based
on the actual tower configuration. This process subsequently enables the establishment of
the global equilibrium equations for the entire system. Prior to the solution of the global
equilibrium equations, boundary conditions such as prescribed displacements must be
incorporated. This procedure facilitates the determination of critical mechanical parameters

including unknown nodal displacements, element stresses, and strains. In transmission
line engineering design, transmission towers are commonly analyzed using spatial truss
models, in which primary and bracing members are idealized as two-force elements, and
joints are assumed to be pinned. The forces acting on members are equivalently transferred
to the nodes. This modeling approach has been widely adopted in current engineering
design codes and related engineering software, as it ensures computational efficiency while
meeting the accuracy requirements of preliminary design [18–20]. Therefore, in this study,
the above truss model is employed in the optimization process to facilitate comparison
with commonly used engineering analysis methods. It should be noted that this model
does not consider bending effects or semi-rigid joint behavior, and their influence will be
further investigated in future work.

the corresponding system of equations unsolvable without additional constraints. There-

points between the transmission tower legs and the foundation, must be introduced to
solve the global nodal stiffness matrix equation.

3.3. Firefly Perturbation Strategy
The Firefly Algorithm [26] is a swarm intelligence optimization algorithm based on
the behavioral patterns of firefly populations. The core concept of the algorithm involves
facilitating global search through a mechanism of brightness comparison and distance-
based attraction among fireflies. Integrating the FA into the traditional SSA enhances the
capacity to escape local optima and improves search precision. This approach is typically
applied to address nonlinear problems and combinatorial optimization tasks.
To address the tendency of the SSA to fall into local optima during structural opti-
mization, a firefly perturbation mechanism is introduced to enhance the local exploration
capability. By simulating the mutual attraction between fireflies, directional perturbations
are applied to individual positions. The fitness values are compared before and after the
perturbation, and the superior one is selected to update the individual status, thereby
enhancing population diversity and improving the global optimization capability [27].

To handle the stress, slenderness ratio, and nodal displacement constraints, a normal-

first transformed into dimensionless relative violation terms, so that constraint quantities
with different physical units can be evaluated in a unified manner. The penalty term is

7. Conclusions
To address the limitations of the SSA in the discrete section optimization of trans-
mission towers, this study introduces Circle chaotic mapping and a firefly perturbation
strategy to improve the algorithm. By integrating the improved algorithm with the finite
element analysis method, an optimization model for the primary load-carrying members
of transmission towers is established. It should be noted that the structural modeling
approach and associated simplifications adopted in this study are mainly intended for the
preliminary sizing optimization of primary members, and the results are primarily used to
evaluate the performance of different optimization algorithms. For practical engineering
applications, further verification is still required at the detailed design stage by employing
refined finite element models, multiple loading conditions, and nonlinear analysis methods
to ensure structural safety and reliability. The main conclusions are as follows:
(1)
To address the deficiencies of SSA at different stages in the discrete section opti-
mization of transmission towers, Circle chaotic mapping and the firefly perturbation
strategy are introduced, thereby enhancing both the global exploration and local
exploitation capabilities of SSA in transmission tower optimization problems.
(2)
A 220 kV cat-head transmission tower is taken as an engineering example. By combin-
ing a MATLAB-based finite element analysis program with ISSA, preliminary sizing
optimization of the primary load-carrying members is performed. The results show
that, under the constraints of member strength, stability, and nodal displacement,
ISSA can obtain a lighter structural scheme, demonstrating its effectiveness for the
preliminary sizing optimization of primary members in the studied tower type.
(3)
Compared with the design results obtained from Daoheng software, the ISSA-
optimized scheme achieves a weight reduction of 152.68 kg, corresponding to a
reduction ratio of approximately 3.79%. Although the reduction ratio for a single
tower is moderate, considering the repetitive construction characteristics of transmis-
sion lines, this result indicates certain practical value in terms of material savings and
engineering applications.
(4)
This study is conducted based on a single tower type and a typical controlling load
case, and some structural components are simplified in the model. Therefore, the
applicability of the proposed method to other tower types, complex load combinations,
and refined engineering models still requires further investigation in future work.
